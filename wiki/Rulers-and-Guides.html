Rulers and guides

Special rulers

Special rulers pull your strokes onto a guide shape as you draw. Pick the Ruler tool, choose a Special Ruler Kind in the options bar, and draw the ruler on the canvas. Its handles reshape it afterwards.

KindStrokes follow
StraightOne straight line
CurveA curve you draw
ConcentricCircles or ellipses around a centre
ParallelLines parallel to the ruler
RadialLines out from a centre (focus lines, sunbursts)
FisheyeArcs through two vanishing points, for fisheye perspective
  • A stroke snaps to whichever guide it's heading along, so you can draw freely near it.
  • Rulers belong to a layer (or to every layer) and are saved in the .pcraft file.
  • View › Special Rulers turns snapping on or off, shows or hides the rulers, and clears them.

Perspective ruler

The perspective ruler sets up one-, two- or three-point perspective. Pen, pencil and eraser strokes snap toward a vanishing point or run upright, so freehand lines come out straight and aimed at the right point.

  • Pick the Ruler tool and choose Perspective in the options bar: 1-Point, 2-Point or 3-Point. Show and Snap sit beside it.
  • With the Ruler tool, drag the vanishing points on the canvas; the eye level follows. Painting tools never grab them, so a stroke can start on one.
  • A stroke picks the closest direction from its first few points, then stays on that line.
  • While a ruler exists, the painting tools' options bar has a snap switch next to symmetry, for flipping between snapping and drawing freely. Shift still draws straight lines in 45° steps.
  • The ruler is saved in the .pcraft file, and placing or moving it can be undone.

1-point: one vanishing point on the eye level. Strokes go toward it, level or upright.

Lucas and Johannes van Doetecum after Hans Vredeman de Vries, Perspective View of a Street, 1560 (National Gallery of Art, CC0).

2-point: two points on the eye level. Strokes go toward either, or upright.

Gustave Caillebotte, Paris Street; Rainy Day, 1877 (public domain).

3-point: adds a third point that upright lines converge on, for looking up or down at tall things.

Skyscrapers from below, photo by Davide Ragusa (CC0, via Wikimedia Commons).

Symmetry

Paint with symmetry across a straight line or along a path; the brush tools' options bar turns it on and sets the axis.

Guides and grids

  • View › Rulers (Ctrl+R) shows the rulers along the canvas edges; drag from them to make guides.
  • Guides, grids and snapping are under View, as in Photoshop.
  • Comic pages come with guides for the trim and safe area.
