The workspace

PIRA's layout is built for painting on a tablet: the canvas in the middle, tools down the left, panels on the right, and the Shortcut Pad on the screen's edge.

Across the top

  • The P button (top left) opens the app menu. The menu bar (File, Edit, Image, Layer, Type, Select, Filter, View, Window, Help) holds every command.
  • The command bar below it has New, Open, Save and the most-used actions.
  • The options bar shows the current tool's settings: for brushes, size, opacity, the pressure toggles and the brush's own settings.

Tools

The tool column is on the left. Painting tools sit on the touch bar as three families:

  • Pen: solid inks with pressure width and steady lines.
  • Pencil: paper grain; pressure darkens and tilt shades.
  • Brush: watercolour, oil, blender and a round mixing brush.

Each button remembers the last brush you used in it, and its picker lists that family's brushes plus your own. See Pens, pencils and brushes.

Under the tools are the foreground and background colours.

Panels

The right column holds Layers, Channels, Swatches, 3D and more, as tabs. Drag a tab to move it, stack it with others or float it. Window shows or hides each panel, and saves layouts as workspaces.

The Shortcut Pad (Android)

A slim strip on the screen's edge, like the ExpressKeys and Touch Ring on a pen tablet.

  • Keys press their shortcut while touched. A modifier key on its own (Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Space) stays held while your finger rests on it, so the pen can Alt-click or Ctrl-click.
  • Double-tap a key to lock it down. It stays held, with a bar along its foot, until you tap it again. Handy for keeping Ctrl or Alt on while the pen clicks layers.
  • The wheel: press it to open a large ring, then slide around it to zoom, rotate the view, size or harden the brush, or step through history. Tap its centre to switch function.
  • Full screen: the key above the keyboard key steps through full screen: first keeping the tool and options bars, then the canvas alone, then back.
  • Keyboard: the bottom key opens the on-screen keyboard; its letters reach PIRA's shortcuts.
  • Window › Shortcut Pad shows or hides it, moves it to either side, and opens its settings: how many keys (2 to 10), each key's shortcut and colour, and the wheel's functions.

Touch gestures (Android)

  • Two fingers pan, pinch to zoom and twist to rotate the view, all at once.
  • A quick two-finger tap undoes; a three-finger tap redoes. You can pick other shortcuts.
  • One finger draws like the pen, moves the canvas, or does nothing: your choice in Window › Touch Input.
  • Palm rejection: fingers are ignored while the pen is down or near the screen.
  • In menus, panels and lists, a flick keeps scrolling, two fingers scroll, and a finger held still right-clicks.

Pen buttons (Android)

The pen's side buttons and eraser end can each erase, right-click, run a shortcut, or do nothing. Set them in Window › Touch Input › Touch Input Settings…. Each has a light that turns green while it's in use, so you can see which button the tablet reports.

Rotating and zooming the view

  • Pinch and twist with two fingers, or use the Shortcut Pad's wheel.
  • The on-canvas compass, a double-click, or Esc puts the rotation back to 0.
  • Fit (bottom right) fits the canvas to the window.
