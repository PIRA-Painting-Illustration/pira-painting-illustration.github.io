Layers

PIRA's layers work like Photoshop's: pixel layers, groups, masks, clipping, blend modes, opacity and fill, adjustment and fill layers, layer styles, text and shape layers, and 3D layers. The Layers panel is in the right column.

The Background layer

A new canvas has a Background layer filled with the paper colour, and an empty Layer 1 above it to draw on (see Starting a canvas). The Background is locked in place at the bottom of the stack, like Photoshop's.

Line art layer settings

PIRA adds four settings for line art and colouring. Each is in Layer and in the Line Art section of the Properties panel, is saved in the .pcraft file, and shows a marker on the layer's row.

SettingWhat it does
Reference LayerMarks the line art that fills and selections read from. The Paint Bucket and Magic Wand can sample only reference layers while you fill on another layer.
Draft LayerFor rough sketches: left out of exports, printing and fills, so you can keep the sketch in the file without it showing up in the result.
Line ColorRecolours the whole layer's lines (blue sketch lines to black, say) without repainting them. You can change or remove it later.
Border EffectDraws an outline around everything on the layer, like a sticker edge.

See Line art and colouring for how they work with the fill tools.

Useful habits

  • Ink on one layer and colour on another layer underneath, with the ink marked as a reference layer.
  • Use clipping (Alt-click between two layers, or Layer › Create Clipping Mask) to shade inside flat colours without going over the edges.
  • Lock transparent pixels to repaint lines without spilling outside them.

Saving layers

.pcraft keeps everything PIRA has, including 3D layers, rulers, timelapse and the line-art settings. PSD keeps the layers Photoshop understands; PIRA-only layers are saved as pixels there. See Files, saving and exporting.
